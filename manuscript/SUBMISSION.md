## What a human must supply

- **Author names, affiliations, corresponding author.** `main.tex` carries
  placeholders that say so.
- **Funding statement, conflict of interest, ethics.** Not drafted; these are
  factual statements only the authors can make.
- **The journal's current formatting requirements.** I have not verified JSSAM's
  present author instructions — word limits, whether review is anonymised, the
  required file layout, or whether a structured abstract is wanted. Check the
  journal page before formatting, and do not assume this template matches.
- **A cover letter.**

## What a referee will attack, and where the paper answers

1. **Originality.** §3 is the contribution: the scale elasticity and the exchange
   identity. The minimax bound in §2 is explicitly *not* claimed as new — the
   equal-variance submodel is the classical intraclass-correlation design
   problem, and §2 says so and cites it.
2. **The theory–application gap.** The guarantee is proved under Assumption R and
   the ESS analysis runs under approximations that Assumption R does not cover.
   §6 states this in a boxed note and §7 calls it the paper's main weakness.
   *This is the most likely ground for a major revision.*
3. **Comparator strength.** Against a component-wise bound given the same
   structure the gain is 0.7–2.3%. §7 concedes it and argues the preference on
   other grounds. §7 also states that no comparison has been run against
   Zhang & Tuoto's split conformal construction, because the targets differ.

## Known debts, all declared in §7

- The $O(1/K)$ GVF bias has no derivation.
- The $1.9/K$ constant in the refined scale law is fitted, not derived.
- Exchangeability under an estimated centre is measured (about $-1/(n_r-1)$) and
  not repaired. Repairing it is the single change that would most improve the
  paper, and it is a new result rather than an edit.
