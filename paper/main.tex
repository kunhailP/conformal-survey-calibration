\section*{Statement of Significance}
A survey estimate is an uncertain description of a population. Using estimates
from many populations to predict another introduces a second source of
uncertainty, the differences between populations, and the two answer different
questions. Methods exist that subtract estimated sampling variance to sharpen
such a prediction. This paper shows that the conditions for using them are
coupled rather than separate: the more sampling error there is to remove, the
harder it is to certify what remains, because the reliability check is
evaluated on the scale the correction shrinks. We give the resulting
requirement in closed form and verify it on European survey data, where it
predicts the method's own decision in every configuration examined while the
fixed sample-size rule it replaces does not.

\section*{Data and Code Availability}
Code, protocols, a claims ledger binding every reported number to the artefact
that produced it, and the outputs of every experiment are in the replication
repository. The simulation of Section~\ref{sec:simulation} requires no
microdata. European Social Survey microdata are distributed under the
provider's access conditions and are not redistributed.

\section*{Acknowledgements and Analysis Planning}
Each experiment has a protocol written and committed before execution, stating
the outcomes that would count against the hypothesis. The sensitivity analysis
of Section~\ref{sec:simulation} was designed after methodological development
and is exploratory; its full grid is reported. One earlier version of the
regional analysis reported a result that a verification run overturned, and the
protocol now requires a reimplementation to reproduce an inherited statistic
before any disagreement with it is reported.
