\subsection{What the correction buys, and whether the anchor holds}
\label{sec:widths}

Two questions remain, and both are answered on the same target rather than
against a benchmark covering a different one.

\paragraph{Realised width against the anchor.} The archive reported the
correction's gain against the \emph{conservative envelope}, and reported a scale
ratio rather than a realised radius. Neither answers what a reader asks, which
is what the correction buys over the band a sensible analyst would otherwise
use. That band is the anchor, and the envelope is a weak comparator: across our
configurations it is \WidConsMed{} times the anchor's radius.

Measured against the anchor at the same level on the same calibration set, the
correction's realised radius is \WidMed{} of it at the median and
\WidLo--\WidHi{} across configurations. Where the gates actually open it is
\WidActLo--\WidActHi, a reduction of \WidGainLo--\WidGainHi{} percent. This sits
inside the oracle ceiling $1-\sqrt{1-\rho^2}$, which the same configurations put
at \WidCeilLo--\WidCeilHi{} percent, the shortfall being the ratio of the two
conformal quantiles that Section~\ref{sec:correction} noted.

The correction is therefore worth having where it is reachable. That changes how
Proposition~\ref{prop:coupling} should be read: the boundary is not a reason to
abandon the correction but the quantity that says where to look for it, and the
answer at these design shares is a narrow band of configurations rather than
none.

\paragraph{Does the anchor hold?} \citet{michal2024model} document that plain
split conformal undercovers under complex survey designs. Our anchor is a
different construction, taking the whole curve of a population as one
exchangeable unit rather than splitting units of a designed sample, but the
concern transfers and is worth testing rather than arguing away.

Calibrating on all other countries' regions and evaluating on the held-out
country's, marginal coverage of the observed target is \AcovMed{} at the median
over \AcovN{} configurations and \AcovLo--\AcovHi{} across them, against a
nominal $0.90$. The largest deviation is below one standard error of a
proportion at these evaluation counts, \AcovSE{} at the smallest. The
finite-sample guarantee for the observed target survives contact with a real
complex design.

Coverage conditional on the held-out country does not, and is not claimed: the
worst country in each configuration runs \AcovWorstLo--\AcovWorstHi. This is the
ordinary marginal-versus-conditional distinction of conformal prediction, and it
is the reason the guarantee is stated over populations rather than for a named
one. A reader who needs a guarantee for one country in particular should use an
area-level construction designed for it \citep{burris2020exact,bersson2024optimal},
not this band.
