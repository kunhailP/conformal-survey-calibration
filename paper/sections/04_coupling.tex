\section{Two gates, one quantity}\label{sec:coupling}

An adaptive implementation of the correction guards it with gates computed from
the calibration set \citep{park2026wrongunit}. Two matter here. A \emph{need}
gate requires the design share to exceed a frozen cutoff, $\rhohat>\rho_0$ with
$\rho_0=0.47$. A \emph{reliability} gate requires
\begin{equation}\label{eq:D}
D=\max_j\frac{\sqrt{2\,s_Y^4(j)/(K-1)+\big(\mathrm{SD}_K(\widehat v^2(j))/\sqrt K\big)^2}}
{\widehat s_G^2(j)}\ \le\ \tau,
\end{equation}
where $\widehat s_G^2$ is the guarded scale, which subtracts a one-sided margin
below the mean design variance so that an uncertain estimate subtracts less. At
the frozen $\tau=0.147$ the implementation reports the requirement $K\ge94$,
obtained by noting $\widehat s_G^2\le s_Y^2$, whence $D\ge\sqrt{2/(K-1)}$.

That floor is correct and, as an account of what blocks the correction,
misleading. The two gates are not independent requirements on two dimensions.

\begin{proposition}[Gate coupling]\label{prop:coupling}
Let $\kappa(j)=\widehat s_G^2(j)/s_Y^2(j)\in(0,1]$ be the scale shrinkage and let
\[
h(j)=\frac{\big(\mathrm{SD}_K(\widehat v^2(j))/\sqrt K\big)^2}
{2s_Y^4(j)/(K-1)+\big(\mathrm{SD}_K(\widehat v^2(j))/\sqrt K\big)^2}
\]
be the share of the squared standard error contributed by dispersion of design
variances across populations. Then at the coordinate attaining the maximum
in~\eqref{eq:D},
\begin{equation}\label{eq:identity}
D=\sqrt{\frac{2}{K-1}}\cdot\frac{1}{\kappa\sqrt{1-h}},
\end{equation}
and under~(S), where the guarded subtraction recovers the design share,
$\kappa\approx1-\rho^2$, so the reliability gate $D\le\tau$ is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:boundary}
K\ \ge\ 1+\frac{2}{\tau^2\,(1-\rho^2)^2\,(1-h)}.
\end{equation}
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Write $A=2s_Y^4/(K-1)$ and $B=(\mathrm{SD}_K(\widehat v^2)/\sqrt K)^2$ at the
maximising coordinate, so $h=B/(A+B)$ and $A+B=A/(1-h)$. Then
$D=\sqrt{A+B}/\widehat s_G^2=\sqrt{A/(1-h)}/(\kappa s_Y^2)
=\sqrt{2/(K-1)}\,/(\kappa\sqrt{1-h})$, which is~\eqref{eq:identity}.
Substituting $\kappa=1-\rho^2$ and solving $D\le\tau$ for $K$
gives~\eqref{eq:boundary}.
\end{proof}

The content is the placement of $\rho$ in~\eqref{eq:boundary}. The
deconvolution shrinks the target scale by $1-\rho^2$, and the diagnostic
divides by that shrunken scale, so the same design share that opens the need
gate inflates the diagnostic by $(1-\rho^2)^{-1}$ and works to close the
reliability gate. \textbf{Design noise large enough to be worth removing is, by
that magnitude, large enough to leave the remainder poorly determined.}

The reported floor of $94$ is~\eqref{eq:boundary} evaluated at $\rho\to0$ and
$h\to0$: it is the requirement in the regime where the correction is not needed.
At the need cutoff itself, $\rho=\rho_0=0.47$, the requirement is already
$K\ge\KreqCut$; at $\rho=0.60$ it is $\KreqSix$, and at $\rho=0.66$, the largest
design share in our applications, $\KreqMax$.

Section~\ref{sec:applications} checks~\eqref{eq:identity}
and~\eqref{eq:boundary} against \RegConfigs{} survey configurations. The
identity holds to \IdErr\% mean and \IdErrMax\% maximum relative error, and the
boundary predicts the gate correctly in \BoundCorrect{} of \RegConfigs{}
configurations, against \BoundNaive{} that the $K\ge94$ rule would admit.
